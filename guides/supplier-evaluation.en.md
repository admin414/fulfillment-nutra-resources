# Evaluate a private-label supplement supplier

A supplier comparison is useful only when the offers describe the same product, order size and destination. Start with one launch scenario instead of comparing headline prices across unrelated offers.

## 1. Define the scenario

Write down the product format, units per order, destination country, intended sales channel and label requirements. Separate a sample, a direct-to-consumer order and a bulk purchase. Each can have different minimums, preparation steps and shipping terms.

## 2. Request product-specific evidence

- Confirm the exact product specification and packaging format.
- Ask which product documents are available and what they actually cover.
- Identify who reviews the artwork, who approves it and which version goes into production.
- Verify any manufacturing or quality claim against the relevant facility, product, date and scope. A badge alone is not evidence.
- Confirm that the product and its proposed presentation are suitable for the intended market with appropriately qualified local support.

Keep the document reference and verification date in your private comparison file. Do not upload confidential documents to a public repository.

## 3. Compare the complete operating cost

Record product cost, label or setup fees, pick-and-pack, shipping, subscriptions, storage when applicable, and the treatment of taxes, duties, failed deliveries and returns. An advertised account price does not describe the total cost of fulfilling an order.

Ask whether a quote depends on stock, weight, packaging, destination or monthly volume. A blank price is an unresolved question, not a zero.

## 4. Verify the fulfillment workflow

Check order acceptance, stock confirmation, payment requirements, dispatch timing and tracking updates. Determine what happens if the address is invalid, the product becomes unavailable, or an order is submitted twice.

For an integration, distinguish a marketing logo from an available connector. Ask for the supported workflow and test evidence. Determine which system owns the order status and how failures are surfaced to the operator.

## 5. Make the decision explicit

Use four statuses: **verified**, **conditional**, **unknown**, **not offered**. Do not turn every unknown into a pass or average a critical blocker into an overall score. Record the next question and responsible person for each unresolved item.
